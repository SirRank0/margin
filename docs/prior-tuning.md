Generated by `scripts/tune-prior.py` (seed 20261111) on 2026-11-11, from
the all-ranks data saved by `scripts/fetch-ability-orders.py --raw-dir`: 44 heroes,
13,677,056 games. The method is in the script's docstring: a random game-level split
(binomial thinning) into two halves, shrinkage fitted on one half, Bernoulli
log-likelihood of the other half's wins and losses at every node.

Log-likelihood is in nats, relative to K = 2,000 (the old value); higher is better.
Held-out games scored: 6,601,829 at strategy nodes and 72,295,067 game-steps at
spend-tree nodes. "Both" is strategy plus spend, the two levels this K governs on
the board's strategy and spend choices. Stability: the board's path built from each
half separately, share of heroes where the two halves agree on the strategy and on
the unlock order, the average share of the 16 click positions that agree, and the
share of heroes where all 16 agree. Each half has half the games, so stability on the
full data is higher than shown; the comparison between K values is what counts.

## Findings

- **One K: 2,000.** It has the best strategy-plus-spend log-likelihood on both splits,
  so PRIOR_K stays at 2,000 (the value it had). The spend-tree nodes, with about 11
  times more held-out game-steps than strategy nodes, decide it.
- **The levels prefer different K**, the same way on both splits: unlock orders do
  best at K = 250 (about +90 nats against 2,000) and strategies at K = 1,000 (about
  +97). Those levels have far more games per option, so they need less pull. A
  separate K per level would fit held-out games slightly better, but at K = 1,000 the
  two halves agree on the strategy less often (59% against 66%). Not adopted here;
  left as a decision for review.
- **MIN_SHARE and SEPARATED_P are unchanged.** This sweep does not test them, so
  there is no evidence to move them.
- Stability rises slowly with K: more pull makes picks agree more often between
  halves, at the cost of fit.
